Gangneung · Sokcho — Gangwon Coast

Where the ridge meets the tide.

Two hours from Seoul by train, this stretch of Korea's east coast holds a 500-year-old scholar's home, a fishing village built by war refugees, and a mountain range that turns the sky red at sunrise. Most guides show you the coffee street. This one shows you why the coast looks the way it does.

Why here

Everyone posts the coffee street.
Almost no one explains the village behind it.

Gangneung and Sokcho get treated as one long Instagram backdrop — Anmok Beach cafés, Sokcho abalone porridge, a cable car photo. All real, all worth doing. But both cities carry stories that don't show up in a five-photo carousel: a Joseon-dynasty household that produced Korea's most famous scholar and the banknote portrait next to him, and a lagoon-side neighborhood built by people who fled the Korean War expecting to go home in a few months and never could. This guide treats those stories as part of the itinerary, not a museum plaque you skip.
