Food

What to eat, and where locals actually go

Both cities have a "front-facing" food scene for visitors and a quieter one for residents. Neither is wrong — but knowing which is which helps you choose on purpose instead of by accident.

Signature dishes by city

DishCityWhat it is
Chodang sundubu (tofu)GangneungSoft tofu made with seawater instead of salt brine — a technique specific to the Chodang neighborhood, served as a simple soft-tofu stew.
MakguksuGangneungBuckwheat noodles in a cold, spicy-savory sauce — a Gangwon-province staple, simpler and earthier than Seoul-style cold noodles.
Abai sundaeSokchoBlood sausage in a squid or intestine casing, a Hamgyong-style dish carried south by Abai Village's original refugee community.
Ojingeo sundaeSokchoSquid stuffed with a rice-and-vegetable filling — a lighter cousin of abai sundae, also rooted in the same Hamgyong food tradition.
Dak-gangjeongGangneung (Jungang Market)Sweet-spicy double-fried chicken that became a market-famous specialty at Gangneung's Jungang Market.

Local vs. tourist-facing

Anmok Coffee Street

Pick one, don't hop five

Every shop looks similar from outside now. Locals tend to favor a handful of the original roasters set back from the main strip rather than the newest beachfront openings — worth asking your accommodation for a current favorite, since turnover is fast.

Abai Village main lane

Walk a block further

The first row of restaurants facing the ferry landing serves the most tourists and prices accordingly. A block or two back, prices drop and the pace slows down.

Sokcho Jungang Market

Go for the stalls, not the seating

Standing food stalls (fried squid, hotteok, fish cake skewers) are where the market actually shines — the sit-down restaurants around its edges are more mixed.

Daepo Port

Buy raw, ask upstairs to prepare it

The common local move: pick fresh seafood from a ground-floor stall, then have it prepared and served in the seating area upstairs, paying a separate small preparation fee.

Dietary notes: vegetarian options are limited outside temple food and some cafés — Sinheungsa Temple near Seoraksan occasionally offers temple-food experiences worth checking in advance. Seafood is central to Sokcho's food identity, so travelers avoiding it will find Gangneung's tofu and noodle dishes easier to build a meal around.
