Seoraksan, sorted by how much time and effort you have

Seoraksan National Park is Korea's third-highest mountain range and its most dramatic, with granite peaks that turn the whole park red and gold in autumn foliage season. It also has routes for almost anyone, from a 30-minute cable car ride to a full-day summit push. Match the route to your day, not the other way around.

~1 hour

Cable car to Gwongeumseong

A cable car near the park's Sogongwon entrance climbs to a ridge near the site of a former fortress, with a short walk up to a viewpoint over the park and coast. The easiest way to get a genuine Seoraksan view without hiking. Lines can be long on weekends and in peak foliage weeks — go early.

2–3 hours

Biseondae Trail

A mostly flat, well-paved trail along a valley to Biseondae Rock, passing waterfalls and pools. Manageable for most fitness levels and doesn't require hiking gear.

4–6 hours

Ulsanbawi Trail

A steeper round trip to a distinctive multi-peaked granite formation, with a long staircase section near the top. Real elevation gain — proper shoes and an early start matter here.

Full day

Daecheongbong summit

The park's highest peak. A serious full-day hike (or overnight with a shelter reservation) for experienced hikers only — this is not a casual add-on to a coastal trip.

Before you go: trail access changes seasonally (some routes close in winter or during dry-season fire-risk periods), and the national park office publishes current conditions and shelter reservation info — worth checking the week of your visit rather than assuming a route is open.
