Sokcho & Seoraksan

Mountain in daylight, history and seafood after dark. Sokcho sits right where Seoraksan National Park meets the sea, which is part of why the city feels different from anywhere else on this coast.

Day 1 — Seoraksan

Morning

Arrive & head straight for the park

Seoraksan gets crowded from mid-morning, especially in autumn foliage season — go early.

Midday

Cable car to Gwongeumseong, or a real trail

Short on time or mobility: the cable car up to Gwongeumseong fortress site gives you the granite-peak view in about 30 minutes round trip. Have a full day: the Biseondae or Ulsanbawi trails are longer, steeper, and genuinely rewarding — check current trail conditions before you commit.

Afternoon

Sinheungsa Temple

Near the park entrance — a working temple with a large bronze Buddha statue outside, easy to fit in on the way out.

Evening

Check in, rest your legs

Save Abai Village for tomorrow — you'll want daylight for the neighborhood, and evening energy for the fish market instead.

Day 2 — Sea & history

Morning

Abai Village

Cross by the hand-pulled ferry (gaetbae) — a small wooden boat pulled across the channel by rope, still the neighborhood's actual way of getting around, not a tourist gimmick. See the full history before you go.

Midday

Lunch in the village

Abai sundae (a Hamgyong-style blood sausage, different from the Seoul version) and squid sundae are the neighborhood's signature dishes.

Afternoon

Sokcho Jungang Market & Daepo Port

Fresh and dried seafood stalls, street food, and a working fishing port — go hungry.

Evening

Depart

Buses to Seoul run from Sokcho's intercity terminal; there's no KTX station in Sokcho itself, so plan the bus timing in advance.
